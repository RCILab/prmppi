Abstract

Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control is widely used in manipulation for its gradient-free, parallel handling of non-convex costs. Manipulation tasks, however, often impose constraints that hold throughout the motion: a closed kinematic chain that two grasping arms keep exactly, or joint limits and obstacle clearances that are never crossed. MPPI handles such constraints only through the cost, as soft penalties that hold approximately and fail under a strong task cost. To address this, we propose Projection-Retraction MPPI (PR-MPPI), which enforces the constraints inside the sampled dynamics. At every rollout step, the sampled velocity is projected to satisfy both constraint types: the equality restricts it to a subspace, and each inequality to a half-space within that subspace, so inequality handling never breaks the equality. This projection, however, satisfies the constraints only to first order, and a finite step leaves a small drift off the equality. Therefore, we retract the returned command back onto the constraint, to numerical tolerance and independent of task weighting. We validate PR-MPPI on 14-DoF dual-arm systems. In simulation, the returned commands satisfy the closed-chain equality to numerical tolerance through a joint-limit stress test and randomized obstacle avoidance. On real hardware, the arms of a Unitree H1-2 humanoid reactively avoid a moving obstacle.

PR-MPPI overview. Sampled control sequences are rolled out through constraint-filtered dynamics. The active constraint filter is applied at every rollout step; after the exponential-weighted MPPI update, the same filter is applied to the first returned input at the measured state, and the resulting finite step is retracted onto the equality manifold.

Contributions

  • We propose PR-MPPI, a constrained sampling-based MPC framework in which a single velocity-space projection carries both constraint types: each active inequality is corrected inside the equality tangent subspace, so inequality handling cannot break the equality regardless of which margins are active.
  • We establish execution-layer guarantees: every retracted command satisfies the equality constraint to the prescribed numerical tolerance independently of the MPPI weighting; under the stated regularity and feasibility conditions, the shifted-margin layer is forward invariant, and a safety buffer satisfying the derived finite-step bound preserves the true inequality margins (Proposition 1).
  • We validate PR-MPPI on a 14-DoF dual-arm Franka system in simulation, where the retracted commands satisfy the closed-chain equality to numerical tolerance while the measured trajectories retain the joint-limit and obstacle margins under aggressive task commands, and demonstrate reactive avoidance of a moving obstacle on the dual arms of a Unitree H1-2 humanoid.

Method

PR-MPPI incorporates equality and inequality constraints into MPPI through projection and retraction. During each sampled rollout, the perturbed velocity is projected onto the intersection of the equality-constraint tangent space and the active inequality half-spaces. After the MPPI update, the first command is projected again at the current configuration, and the resulting finite-step equality drift is removed by retracting the integrated configuration onto the constraint manifold.

Problem Definition

The control input is the joint velocity. Over the prediction horizon, MPPI minimizes the expected terminal, running, and control-effort costs while enforcing a configuration-space equality c(q) = 0 and inequality margins h(q) ≥ 0 under stochastic control perturbations. At every control cycle, perturbed control sequences are rolled out, assigned trajectory costs, and combined by the standard exponential-weighted MPPI update.

Constraint-Aware Sampled Dynamics

Instead of treating constraints only through penalties, PR-MPPI filters every sampled velocity before it enters the rollout dynamics. The equality constraint defines the manifold M = {q : c(q) = 0}. A sampled velocity is first mapped into the tangent space using the null-space projector N(q) = I - Jc†(q)Jc(q). The pseudoinverse is computed with a rank-truncated SVD so that the projector and restoration corrections remain bounded near kinematic singularities.

Inequality margins are shifted by a positive safety buffer, h̄(q) = h(q) - hsafe, and expressed as first-order control-barrier-function half-spaces. Margins that violate the half-space or lie within an activation band form the rollout active set. The filtered velocity is the minimum-norm projection onto the intersection of the equality tangent space and all active inequality half-spaces. Because each inequality correction is premultiplied by the equality null-space projector, the correction remains inside the equality tangent space.

Execution-Layer Projection and Retraction

The exponential-weighted MPPI average is formed from the sampled inputs, so the first updated virtual input has not itself passed through the constraint filter. Before actuation, PR-MPPI applies the same equality-and-inequality projection at the measured configuration. The projected velocity satisfies the equality only to first order; integrating it over a finite time step leaves a second-order residual. PR-MPPI therefore initializes the integrated state and applies a truncated Gauss-Newton retraction until the retained equality residual falls below the prescribed tolerance. The resulting retraction map produces the final commanded configuration on the constraint manifold.

Execution-Layer Guarantees

Under the regularity, feasibility, and local-convergence conditions stated in Proposition 1, every retracted command satisfies the equality constraint to the prescribed numerical tolerance independently of the task cost, MPPI temperature and covariance, and inequality-handling parameters. The proposition also establishes a finite-step bound for the shifted inequality margin, forward invariance of a thin shifted-margin layer, and a sufficient safety buffer for preserving the true inequality margins.
